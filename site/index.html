Kage.

Turn manga pages into cinematic motion comics.

A deterministic rendering pipeline, with computer vision for framing and generative models for narration and animation. Kage reads your pages, finds the panels, decides how to film them, and renders an MP4 with camera moves, depth parallax and a synthesised score — on your machine, with no upload, no queue and no account.

How it works

Four steps, and you can change any of them

  1. 01Add pages

    Drop in a PDF or page images. Kage trims the camera background and margins off a photographed book, splits two-page spreads, and finds the panels.

  2. 02Choose a look

    A style sets the grade, the camera vocabulary, the pacing and the score together. Kage Noir holds and pushes in; Shonen Kinetic cuts every 1.6 seconds.

  3. 03Review shots

    The whole sequence is one editable table — length, camera move, transition, effects, sound cues — with a thumbnail of the panel each shot is framed on.

  4. 04Render

    Frames are generated one at a time, scored, and muxed into an MP4. A 30-second film takes about a minute on a mid-range laptop.

What it does to the artwork

It moves the page, not just a crop over it

Depth parallax

A depth model estimates how far away each pixel is, so near things travel further than far ones. The background holds still and the foreground slides across it — the cue your eye actually reads as depth.

Framing that finds the drawing

A page is mostly paper. Kage scores every window by how much ink and detail it holds, frames the shot there, and fences the camera so a pan can never park on an empty margin or a panel flooded with black.

A model picks the pages

CLIP ranks pages by how much artwork they carry, so covers and walls of text are skipped. It takes a continuous run rather than the best pages from across a volume, so the result is one scene in order instead of a gallery.

Narration, if you want it

A vision model describes each panel and a language model writes one line per shot, inside a word budget set by that shot's length. It can only use nouns from what was actually seen. Every line is editable.

What it is not. No frame is drawn and no character is animated. Kage is a motion comic engine: it makes your artwork move, it does not make new artwork. Image-to-video can add generated camera motion to a few hero shots, but there are no in-betweens and no redrawn cels.

For developers

Run it from source

git clone https://github.com/spike-crypto/Kage
cd Kage
uv venv
uv pip install -r requirements.txt
python launcher.py

FFmpeg is required — drop ffmpeg.exe and ffprobe.exe into tools/bin/, or have them on your PATH. Run python scripts/check_environment.py and it will tell you what is missing.

Everyone is welcome

Built in the open, for people who love this stuff

Come and work on it

Kage is open source and open to anyone. If you love manga and anime and want to help give these stories motion, there is a place for you here — whether you write Python, understand how a scene should be cut, or just know when a shot feels wrong. No invitation needed and no experience required. Open an issue, send a pull request, or say what you would change.

Everything is measured and written down, so you can see why each decision was made before you change it. If you disagree with one, bring a measurement and change it.

Where help is most wanted

Read the dialogue. Speech bubbles are visible in almost every frame and nothing reads them yet. This is the single biggest thing standing between Kage and telling a real story.

Better panel detection. Bleeds, overlaps and diagonal gutters still defeat it.

Reading order. Right-to-left is assumed; nothing detects which a book is.
